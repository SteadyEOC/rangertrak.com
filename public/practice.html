Ways to practice

Learn it

The Tour

Three tours on one page, hands on, in the app itself. Tour A, Basics, in Easy menus, on the Grand Canyon demo, so it never touches a real mission. Tour B, Advanced, in Full menus: set up a mission, the roster and places, tactical calls and the Setup file, every coordinate format, and wrap-up. Tour C, Enthusiasts: RangerView, field phones and several command posts.

Every call card is a real exchange with prowords (THIS IS, OVER, ROGER, I SPELL), and every leg names its seats: scribe, net control, field team and observer. Swap seats at each rest stop. A Reference tab holds radio basics and the demo cast; Feedback holds the checklist and the only Print button.

The Field Guide

The Field Guide is the long read: every screen, set up, used and wrapped up, with pictures. Print it for the go-box.

Help on every page

Press Help at the top of any page in the app for what that page does. The full Help page holds setup, your data, the FAQ and Contact us, and always matches the version you’re running.

Practice missions

Demo missions

Built-in practice missions, such as an overdue hiker at the Grand Canyon, for training or showing someone the app. Fictional people, real ground. The two largest are modeled on real events: the 2018 Montecito debris flows (about 400 reports, 30 teams) and the 2014 SR 530 landslide near Oso, Washington (about 500 reports, 40 teams, with ICS-213 messages). While one is loaded, Details on the Entry page’s demo card opens what really happened.

Loading one replaces what’s on the device, so try it in a spare browser or back up first.

Exercise mode

The icon at the left of the status pill in the header shows whether the mission is a Demo, an Exercise or an Incident; hover it to change it. An exercise stamps EXERCISE on the printed ICS-213s, the Form 309 Communications Log, the After Action report and the map sheet, so a drill’s paper can’t be mistaken for a real log.

Start the next mission

After the drill, Start the next mission (Mission, Danger zone) clears the radio log and the mission name but keeps the roster, places and tactical calls unless you tick them, so the real thing starts clean. It shows what it will clear, offers a backup first, and the Journal records it.

Run a drill

A one-hour drill from the Tour

You need a laptop, two radios (or two phones), and three or four people. Use Tour B’s call cards and seats: one scribe, one net control, rangers walking a few blocks and calling in. Vashon ACS ran a one-hour cut of it at its November 2026 exercise. Write down every place someone gets stuck, and tell us.

The exercise console (for trainers)

A free, offline controller’s console at rangertrak.org/exercise. Open a scenario (a Montecito sample is built in), pick the teams your class plays, and it runs the clock, shows cue cards to read out, pauses at decision points and drives a room display. Background traffic arrives as catch-up files the app loads like any Report Packet. Afterwards it checks the class’s exported log against the script.

Open the RangerTrak app once in that browser, then the console once while online; after that rangertrak.org/exercise/console.html, the room display and the sample open with no Internet. No accounts, no keys, no AI.
