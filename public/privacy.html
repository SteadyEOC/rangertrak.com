Privacy

I’m John Cornelison, KE7KDQ. I build RangerTrak on my own, with no company behind it. Here is what happens to what you put in it.

Last updated October 18, 2026

The short answer: your mission stays on your device. No accounts, and nothing is ever sent to me. When online, report locations go to OpenStreetMap to fill in the “Derived address”, with no names attached. Everything that leaves the device is listed below.

Where your data lives

  • Everything you enter is saved in the browser on the device you’re using. It isn’t on a server. I never see it, and I can’t recover it for you.
  • The roster can hold names, phone numbers, addresses, call signs and photos. It is unencrypted unless you turn on device encryption (below).
  • Browsers, iPhones especially, can clear stored data. Back up; Help explains how to make it less likely.
  • On iPhone and iPad, the Home Screen app keeps its own copy, separate from Safari. Install first, then set up.

Device encryption

Optional, at Mission → Data safety. It encrypts the roster, reports and photos (roster photos and attached files) with a passphrase; places and mission settings stay unencrypted. It protects a lost or shared device, not one left unlocked, and the app doesn’t lock itself when idle. A forgotten passphrase can’t be recovered: you erase and restore from a backup.

Photos, video and audio

A report can carry photos, video or audio. They are stored in the browser like everything else, and device encryption covers them. Treat them like the roster:

  • A photo or video can show faces, homes, vehicles and license plates.
  • An original file keeps the camera’s hidden details (EXIF), often including the exact GPS position where it was taken. RangerTrak keeps originals exactly as they arrived, so a copy can be shown to be identical.
  • A smaller copy, offered when a file is attached, drops those details, and so do the small pictures in lists and printouts.
  • Files go only where you send them: with a phone’s Report Packet, in a backup’s attachments file, in a hand-over file, or to RangerView if you switch its attachments on.

Files you make

A mission backup can take a passphrase; left blank, it’s plain text. It also holds a Google API key and the RangerView address, if you set them. When reports have attachments, the backup writes a second file beside it with the photos and videos; the same passphrase locks both. Exports, Setup files and Report Packets are always plain, and a Report Packet is sent by whichever app you pick (text, email). You choose where these files go, so treat them as confidential.

What leaves the device: the complete list

  1. Address lookups, when online. Typed addresses, and the coordinates of each report, go straight from your browser to OpenStreetMap’s Nominatim (or to Google, if you add a Google key). No names or notes go with them. Nominatim sees the location and your Internet (IP) address; I see nothing. Offline, nothing is sent.
  2. Map images, when online. From OpenStreetMap, OpenTopoMap, Esri (terrain shading, the roads-and-labels overlay, and the satellite view if you pick it), the U.S. Geological Survey’s National Map (the aerial-photo base layer and the shaded-relief overlay), and Waymarked Trails if you turn on hiking trails. They see your IP address and the map area, as with any web map. Save this area downloads the same kind of images ahead of time, for offline use, from the same services (Esri’s layers are never saved). Offline map files come from rangertrak.org or from a file you load.
  3. Feedback, when you send it. It becomes a public issue on GitHub, so keep mission data out of it. Your IP address is used briefly to stop repeat sends and isn’t kept.
  4. RangerView, only if you turn it on. A small, private mini-website on your team’s own laptop. It receives the radio log and messages, never the roster, so others on the command post’s WiFi can read them. It answers only on the local network, never a public address. You can give it a password; without one, anyone on that WiFi can read it. Attachments go to it only if you switch that on. It keeps nothing once stopped.

The app’s own Log page stays on the device and clears on reload, but it can hold report details. Don’t paste it into feedback.

The websites

rangertrak.org (the app) and rangertrak.com (this site) run on Cloudflare, which sees each visitor’s IP address, like any host, and keeps logs briefly. Visits are counted with Cloudflare’s cookie-free analytics, which doesn’t track individual visitors and never sees what you type into the app. This site sets no cookies and loads nothing from other sites.

What RangerTrak doesn’t do

No accounts, ads, tracking cookies, or selling or sharing your data. No background location tracking: GPS is read only in field mode and the “near me” demo, after the browser asks you. It isn’t aimed at children.

Deleting your data

Clear the browser’s site data for rangertrak.org; that removes everything, including saved maps. Uninstalling doesn’t always do it: iPhone and iPad do, Chrome and Edge ask, and Android varies. Files you exported or sent are out of the app’s reach, so delete them wherever they went.
